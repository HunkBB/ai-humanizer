# Appointment counts and continuity in outpatient listings

The proposed study examines whether stable counts of published outpatient appointments coexist with changes in the appointment times listed. It concerns twelve clinics in a fictional region that publish weekly scheduling snapshots, with an observation period of eight weeks and the clinic-week as the unit of analysis. Permission to download historical snapshots remains pending; no data have been obtained or analyzed.

Continuity is defined as the proportion of slots listed at a baseline snapshot that remain listed at the next weekly snapshot. A slot is identified by clinic, specialty, appointment date and start time. For each pair of consecutive snapshots, both the baseline denominator and the retained-slot numerator are restricted to appointment dates later than both snapshot dates. This restriction prevents appointments that have already elapsed from being treated as removed listings. The proposed comparison considers changes in listed appointment counts alongside continuity, allowing the analysis to distinguish numerical stability from persistence in the specific times shown.

Missing snapshots remain missing observations. Pairs with inconsistent specialty definitions are excluded, with the number of excluded clinic-week pairs and the reasons for exclusion reported. These rules preserve the distinction between missing information and changes in published listings.

The study is descriptive: no causal effect is identified, and listings cannot establish actual booking success or completed visits. Its proposed contribution is a comparison of appointment counts and slot continuity within the observed listings; it makes no claim about patient-level access. Raw-data access remains a condition of feasibility.
