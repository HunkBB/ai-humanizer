# Academic editing examples by genre

These are invented teaching examples, not quotations from Wikipedia or facts about a real applicant. Use the reasoning, not fixed replacement sentences. Apply a substantive detail only if the task supports it. Formal examples below follow this project's preference to avoid `would`, `will`, and their contractions in newly written academic body text; this is not a universal academic rule. Before/bad examples deliberately retain wording under review. Protect actual quotations and titles.

## Formal proposals and papers

| Problem | Before | A possible edit | Meaning to protect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inflated opening | In today's rapidly evolving healthcare landscape, provider access plays a pivotal role in shaping equitable outcomes. | The proposed study examines whether similar changes in physician counts correspond to different changes in network membership. | A stated research question is not an established finding or an effect on equity. Retain the actual question if it differs. |
| Stock contrast | This project is not merely about counting doctors, but about unlocking a deeper understanding of access. | The proposed comparison concerns both physician counts and the identities of listed physicians. | Listings do not establish appointments or patient access. Define the comparison in the methods. |
| Ceremonial promise | The study will yield comprehensive, actionable and transformative insights. | The planned output is an estimate of persistent absence from provider directories. | Name the intended output, not a guaranteed finding or policy benefit; define persistence separately. |
| Unnecessary nominalization | The implementation of an assessment of quarterly stability will be undertaken. | The planned analysis assesses quarterly stability. | Retain the analytical target and proposed status. Do not invent a dataset or substitute a different operation. |
| Dense definition | The estimand constitutes the proportion of baseline NPIs exhibiting sustained non-observation over two subsequent quarterly snapshots. | The primary measure is the proportion of physicians listed in the baseline quarter whose NPIs are absent from both subsequent quarterly files. | This defines a proposed measure; it reports no result. Preserve the baseline denominator, both follow-up periods, identifier and any cross-specialty checks. Directory absence is not contract termination. |
| Conversational transition | Practice-site changes need a separate check. | Changes in reported practice locations require separate assessment. | Retain the reason and method for that assessment in the paragraph. This sentence alone does not explain them. |
| Generic conclusion | Despite challenges, this project has considerable potential to inform the future of healthcare. | Feasibility depends on consistent physician and network identifiers across quarters. | State the actual feasibility condition and retain the response to failed linkage; do not substitute a new study. |
| Empty interpretation of completed work | I cleaned provider records, demonstrating the transformative potential of data-driven decision-making. | I checked whether duplicate provider records affected the counts. | Use the concrete task only if it occurred. Otherwise remove the unsupported interpretation without inventing an activity. |
| Source-existence narration | The faculty website identifies Professor Lee as a researcher associated with insurance policy. | Professor Lee studies insurance policy. | Verify the profile; retain attribution if the identity or scope is disputed. For research fit, explain the relevant finding and its connection to the proposed question. |

## Calibrate register without inflating it

All three columns illustrate style choices. A formal revision still needs the surrounding evidence and methodological detail.

| Context | Conversational or loose | Appropriate academic expression | Overwritten: avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal: purpose | I want to find out if the same total hides different names. | The research question concerns changes in physician membership within networks with stable physician counts. | The investigation endeavors to elucidate the latent compositional reconfiguration concealed by aggregate numerical invariance. |
| Paper: association | Coverage and visits seem to go together, but that does not tell us what causes what. | The observed association between coverage and visits does not establish a causal effect. | The observed associational configuration is insufficient for the establishment of definitive causal attribution. |
| Proposal: conditional access | I would use linked claims data if access were granted. | The proposed use of linked claims data is contingent on access approval. | The operationalization of linked claims utilization is predicated upon the conferment of accessibility authorization. |

The middle column is not a vocabulary substitution list. Keep a simple word when it is exact; retain terms such as `estimate`, `association`, `denominator`, and `identification` when they identify the intended concept. Do not strengthen “may reflect” to “demonstrates” merely to sound decisive.

## Develop an analytical paragraph

**Loose proposal transition:** “Professor Lee's study gives this project a useful starting point. It looks at network size, and I want to look at the names too.”

**Formal proposal, only where the source supports the premise:** “Lee's analysis relates network size to service use. Physician counts, however, do not describe changes in the identities of listed physicians. The proposed study therefore examines network membership alongside changes in network size. Its scope is descriptive: directory changes alone do not establish changes in patient access.”

The revision makes the inferential bridge explicit while retaining scope limits. Do not infer that Lee ignored membership, claim a literature gap, or add a causal conclusion unless the sources establish it. This is a paragraph-development example, not a mandatory four-sentence structure.

## Outreach and personal statements

Direct address and a visible author can suit these genres. Keep outreach professional; do not carry its conversational framing into every methods paragraph.

| Genre and purpose | Before | A possible edit | Meaning to protect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outreach: research fit | Your groundbreaking body of work profoundly resonates with my passion for impactful policy. | Your study prompted my interest in whether physician counts capture changes in network membership. | Use only if the personal connection is confirmed. Otherwise describe the intellectual connection without inventing motivation. |
| Outreach: request | I would love to hear your thoughts on this. | I welcome your assessment of the feasibility of this question. | A request for discussion does not imply a prior relationship or supervision offer. |
| Statement: completed experience | My experience equipped me with a robust analytical toolkit and transferable competencies. | At the insurer, I grouped customers by claims patterns and examined differences within the overall average. | Preserve verified individual contribution; do not invent a task or a retrospective lesson. |
| Any submission: chat residue | Here is a polished version you can send. Let me know if you would like it more formal. | Remove from submission prose. | Keep authentic AI-use disclosure where required; removing editorial residue does not erase AI's actual role. |

## Preserve tense, conditions and authorial choices

- **Planned work:** “I will compare quarterly files” can become “The proposed method involves comparing quarterly files.” Do not change it to “Quarterly files were compared.”
- **Completed work:** “We compared quarterly files” remains past tense if the comparison occurred. Formality does not require impersonal rewriting.
- **Conditional work:** “I would use the linked records only if approval were granted” can become “The proposed use of linked records requires prior approval.” Preserve any additional feasibility conditions.
- **Uncertain interpretation:** “The result may reflect reporting delays” needs no change if it accurately expresses uncertainty. Do not move the uncertainty into a private editing note.
- **Useful first person:** “I propose estimating directory stability before evaluating its relationship with utilization” is compatible with formal prose when this is the author's actual proposal. Retain it unless the task's genre or explicit preference requires another construction.
- **Protected quotations:** Preserve exact source wording even when it contains a disfavored word; record the quotation as a justified exception rather than silently altering it.

## Patterns to retain and overcorrections to reject

- Retain “The estimate was not statistically significant” where necessary. Do not remove `not` or `significant` to satisfy a vocabulary heuristic.
- Retain “Coverage was associated with visits” for an observational association. Do not strengthen it to “coverage increased visits.”
- Retain “We measured enrollment, spending and visits” if the three outcomes were measured; neither first person nor a three-part list is intrinsically defective.
- Retain the distinction between listing changes and contract termination, useful limitations headings, precise tables, and punctuation that clarifies relationships.
- Repeat an exact construct such as “persistent directory removal” instead of rotating among `turnover`, `attrition`, `displacement`, and `exit` when their meanings differ.
- Reject choppy rewrites such as “I checked counts. Counts changed. That matters.” Preserve the relationships between operations and interpretations in connected prose.
- Reject indiscriminate passive voice, longer words, noun chains, fixed sentence lengths, modal substitutions, or first-person quotas. Preserve a clear formal paragraph when it already meets the task.
