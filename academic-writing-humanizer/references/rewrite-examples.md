# Original academic editing examples

These are invented teaching examples, not quotations from Wikipedia or facts about a real applicant. Use the reasoning, not fixed replacement sentences. An edit is acceptable only when its factual assumptions are supported by the actual task.

## Patterns to retain when they carry meaning

- “The estimate was not statistically significant” can be necessary. Do not remove “not” or “significant” to satisfy a vocabulary heuristic.
- “Coverage was associated with visits” can correctly interpret an observational analysis. Do not strengthen it to “coverage increased visits.”
- “We measured enrollment, spending and visits” has three distinct outcomes. Keep the list if all three are part of the study.
- “The measure captures listing changes rather than contract termination” distinguishes the observable construct from an unsupported interpretation. Rephrasing is optional; the distinction must survive.
- “The result may reflect reporting delays” is substantive uncertainty. Do not make it definite or put it only in private notes.
- A required “Limitations” heading, precise methods table, and useful em dash can remain.
- Repeating “persistent directory removal” is preferable to drifting among “turnover,” “attrition,” “displacement” and “exit” if they imply different events.

## Do not overcorrect

Bad revision: “I checked counts. Counts changed. That matters. I will investigate.” It is short but choppy and vague.

Better, where supported: “I will compare quarterly counts with the identities behind them. This will show whether a stable total can conceal changes in which physicians are listed.” The relation between operations is clear; there is no need for a sentence-length target.
