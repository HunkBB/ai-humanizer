# Research checks: B, C, D, E, G

## B — Background

Use the fact ledger. Include experience needed to explain preparation. Distinguish transferable skills from completed work in the field: insurance marketing analysis is not Medicaid research; coursework exposure is not proven applied mastery. Describe learning needs without invented deficits or achievements.

## C — Question and contribution

State one primary question and the kind of answer the project can support. For quantitative empirical work, specify population, geography, time, comparison/exposure, outcome, unit and intended inference; define a descriptive estimand when causality is unsupported. For qualitative, theoretical or interpretive work, use the appropriate cases/materials, selection logic, analytic approach and contribution instead of forcing an exposure/outcome template. Frame proposed mechanisms as hypotheses.

Compare at least two relevant checked works when feasible. Specify what changes: measurement, mechanism, design, population, time horizon or decision use. Avoid “first” or “no research exists” without an adequate search. A new location alone does not establish novelty. Label targeted searches accurately.

## D — Feasibility

Use the rows relevant to the design. Adapt data access to recruitment, archives or source materials where appropriate; do not force quantitative identifiers, weights or causal identification onto another research tradition.

| Element | Questions |
|---|---|
| Data | Dataset/version/years/variables? Public or restricted? Who has access? What was actually inspected? |
| Population | Inclusion/exclusion, stable identifiers, geography and denominator? |
| Measures | Direct observation or proxy? Comparable definitions across time? |
| Design | Why this design? What assumptions support the intended inference? |
| Missingness | True absence, reporting change, selective coverage or linkage error? |
| Analysis | Primary comparison, uncertainty, clustering where relevant, sensitivity checks? |
| Causality | Exogenous variation? Confounding, selection, spillovers or timing concerns? |
| Resources | Pilot, data agreements, institutional review, budget, timing and training? |

For DiD, justify comparison and timing; passing a pre-trend test is not proof. For IV, discuss relevance, exclusion, independence and target population; a variable's name does not establish validity. Separate prediction from causality and address leakage.

For provider directories, distinguish listed participation, active participation, accepting new patients, appointment access, visits, quality and patient continuity. Absence from a file is not a verified contract termination. Compare complete snapshots, deduplicate clinician identifiers, account for locations/products and prescribe missing-snapshot handling. Do not infer a current schema from an old codebook.

If a necessary dataset is unavailable, propose a bounded feasible first stage. Mark scope changes substantive. Never imply access that is merely hoped for.

## E — Professor fit

Read an official profile and 2–3 relevant primary works where possible. Check current position but do not infer recruitment status. Record each work's question, setting, design, result and limitation. Connect an actual design choice; generic praise or repeating an abstract does not establish fit.

Separate topic, method and applicant preparation. Never invent conversations, encouragement, mentorship, collaboration or supervision agreement. Keep test drafts separate from actual correspondence.

## G — Source ledger

Record central claim, URL/DOI, bibliographic identity, source type, checked date, access level, supporting section and limits. Verify identity and support separately. Use honest access labels: abstract-only, full-text, official documentation, indexed excerpt or unverified.

Prefer original research and official data documentation. Check author/year/title/venue/DOI, quotations, in-text/reference correspondence, numerical denominators and generalizability. Do not treat a real citation as support for an unrelated claim. Omit nonessential unverifiable claims or flag them. DOI extraction by a script is not verification.
